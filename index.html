/ 01 — The problem

Concussion care is broken at both ends of the injury.

Diagnosis is subjective. Clearance is subjective. And the moment that carries the most risk and the most liability, the return-to-play or return-to-work decision, still relies on the same self-reported symptom checklist that failed at the sideline.

At the point of injury

Diagnosis depends on self-report.

Sideline protocols lean on symptom reporting and observer judgment. Athletes underreport to stay in the game. Preseason baseline tests can be sandbagged so a post-injury comparison looks benign.

At the point of clearance

Return-to-play is where second-impact risk lives.

Graduated return-to-play and return-to-work protocols are anchored to the same subjective reporting, at exactly the moment institutional liability concentrates and the cost of getting it wrong is highest.

/ 02 — The product

One measurement engine. Two clinical surfaces.

Speech production is one of the most neurologically demanding motor tasks a person performs. Mild traumatic brain injury measurably disrupts articulation, timing, coordination, prosody, and fluency, and unlike blood draws, EEG caps, or eye-tracking rigs, voice can be captured anywhere, in under a minute, at near-zero marginal cost.

01

Baseline enrollment

A short standardized voice protocol, sustained vowels, syllable repetition, a reading passage, spontaneous speech, recorded once at intake or preseason. Because the system measures each person against their own baseline, it sidesteps population-norm variability and is far harder to sandbag than cognitive baseline tests.

02

Detection screen

At a suspected injury, a sub-60-second sample is compared against baseline and scored. It's decision support, a flag that triggers removal and formal assessment, not a replacement for clinical diagnosis.

03

Recovery monitoring

From injury onward, a 30-second sample every day, from home, renders an objective recovery trajectory against baseline and flags plateaus, regressions, and convergence back to normal.

The flagship surface

Every other concussion tool is episodic. Voice is continuous.

Blood, EEG, and eye tracking capture a moment. None of them can produce a daily objective recovery curve, because their cost, hardware, and setting requirements make daily measurement impossible. Voice complements the point-of-injury tools and owns the recovery arc outright, which is where the clearance decision lives, where the liability lives, and where the budget lives.

/ 03 — The science

Built on a decade of vocal biomarker research.

Machine learning studies of concussion speech have consistently identified discriminative temporal features (duration, rhythm, zero-crossing rate) and spectral features (MFCCs, spectral flatness and bandwidth), with newer work applying deep learning directly to speech spectrograms. Vocal biomarkers already show efficacy in cognitive impairment, Parkinson's, and ALS, adjacent validation for the premise that voice carries clinically meaningful neurological signal.

HONEST FRAMING

Published concussion-specific voice datasets remain small by machine learning standards, and confounds, fatigue, exertion, dehydration, emotion, ambient noise, are real. No voice-based concussion diagnostic has FDA clearance today. The field is validated enough to build on and immature enough that the winner will be decided by whoever assembles the best longitudinal dataset.

REGULATORY PATH

FDA has already cleared objective concussion-assessment aids across other modalities, EEG-based devices, eye-tracking diagnostics, and a blood biomarker cartridge, establishing that the agency accepts objective aids to concussion assessment. Speakwell launches as a clinical workflow and monitoring tool, clinician stays the decision-maker, while it builds the evidence base for a defined-claim clearance.

Status

Speakwell is currently a research and clinical-workflow tool. It records, quantifies, and displays voice metrics over time and does not make diagnostic claims. It is not yet FDA cleared and is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease.
